STRUCTURE / OUTLINE
overall_arc: Nora travels to Thorn to confront Silas, discovers he manipulated her path and trapped her, actively resists him, forms a forced alliance when Orlov agents arrive, learns the full extent of his betrayal, separates when he gives her a real choice to leave, then returns on her own terms to defeat the Orlov threat and begin a relationship based on explicit consent and chosen terms. The curse remains unbroken.
Romance arc in full:
Stage 1: The hunter – Nora discovers Silas's name in payments and travels to confront him. She believes he is the person hired to deliver her.
Stage 2: The trap – Silas admits accepting the contract, takes her phone, and prevents her from leaving during the storm. She learns he anticipated the third attack.
Stage 3: Active war – Nora attempts escape, searches the closed rooms, tries to contact the mainland, withholds information, and turns Rowan's trust and island knowledge into leverage. Silas responds with tighter control.
Stage 4: Forced alliance – Orlov agents reach Thorn. Nora and Silas must cooperate. Each saves the other for strategic reasons before either admits emotional investment.
Stage 5: Unwanted recognition – Nora sees Silas with Rowan and understands his capacity for loyalty. Silas recognises that Nora's resistance is not recklessness but a defence of personhood.
Stage 6: Desire without absolution – They become physically drawn to each other while the contract and third attack remain unresolved. Nora explicitly refuses to confuse desire with trust.
Stage 7: The deeper betrayal – Nora learns the full extent of Silas's manipulation, including that he allowed the intruder to reach her and chose Thorn as her destination before she knew his name.
Stage 8: Separation – Silas gives Nora access to transport, evidence, and the choice to leave. He does not follow, restrain, or engineer another reason for her to remain.
Stage 9: Choice – Nora returns or stays for her own strategic and emotional reasons. She leads the final plan against the Orlovs and fights beside Silas rather than being saved by him.
Stage 10: HEA for Book 1 – The Orlov threat is defeated or transformed, Nora is no longer Silas's captive, and their relationship begins under explicit chosen terms. The curse remains unbroken. They choose not to let fear make the decision for them.

PART C: TROPES, CRAFT RULES, AND ARC (FULL DETAILS)
Primary tropes
Genuine enemies-to-lovers – Nora has evidence that Silas accepted payment to deliver her. He manipulated her route, allowed danger to reach her, took her phone, and prevents her leaving. She actively resists him.
Forced proximity – The island, storms, vanished transport, hostile sea, and Silas's restrictions keep them together. The supernatural environment complicates escape but must not replace Silas's responsibility for trapping her.
Captor and captive tension – Use carefully. Nora retains practical agency, access to a weapon at key moments, investigative goals, and the ability to challenge Silas. The story must never romanticise the absence of consent as proof of devotion.
Morally grey hero – Silas kills, lies, tracks, coerces, and manipulates. He is not soft underneath. His tenderness toward Rowan reveals capacity, not innocence.
Gothic romance – Isolated house, family curse, inherited violence, closed rooms, hostile landscape, portraiture, secrecy, and desire intertwined with dread.
Craft rules (full list)
Active resentment early – Nora does not merely distrust Silas. She confronts him, wounds him, attempts escape, searches his belongings, challenges his authority, and uses information against him.
Preserve moral conflict – Do not reveal that every harmful act was secretly harmless. Silas truly did manipulate her. Later context can complicate the act but cannot erase it.
Hero's wound revealed gradually – His father's murder, the revenge killing, the curse, Rowan's history, and the origin of the limp are separate reveals.
Heroine has an independent objective – Nora gathers evidence against the Orlovs, seeks communication with the mainland, studies the contract, and plans a life beyond Silas.
Attraction before trust – Physical and emotional attraction may appear while Nora still considers him an enemy. Desire is not forgiveness.
Banter amid darkness – Use dry humor selectively. Do not make every response a polished line. Silence, bluntness, fear, and failed jokes create variation.
External conflict is constant – The Orlov hunters, the contract, incoming boats, compromised contacts, winter isolation, and the mystery of why Nora is wanted alive drive chapter-level action.
Spice is emotional exposure – Intimacy must expose questions of control, consent, observation, and trust. Explicit scenes occur only when Nora has meaningful freedom to choose and can stop the encounter without retaliation.
Short chapters with hooks – Each chapter ends with a threat, revelation, reversal, or emotional decision.
Human prose – Avoid repeated patterns such as "the way people," "the kind of," "specific," and constant personification of the island or house. Do not give Nora a quip in every exchange. Vary sentence length and interiority.
PART E: TONE AND SPICE
Darkness: 4 out of 5. Violence is present but not relentlessly graphic. At least one killing occurs on page and is witnessed without prolonged gore.
Psychology: Heavy. No complete redemption and no cure-through-love. Nora does not heal Silas. Silas does not rescue Nora into dependence. They build a relationship while retaining damage, conflict, and accountability.
Atmosphere: Gothic tension rather than full horror. The house and island should create pressure, not overwhelm the romance and human conflict.
Humor: Dry, internal, ironic. Never broad comic relief. Reduce humor during genuine terror, grief, betrayal, and intimacy.
Heat level: Steamy and explicit at relationship milestones. Approximately 70 to 80 percent tension, plot, atmosphere, and investigation, with 20 to 30 percent romance and spice.
Consent rule: No explicit sexual contact while Nora is physically unable to leave, believes refusal will endanger her, or lacks enough information to make a meaningful decision. Tension can exist during captivity. Consummation must follow a real shift in power.

CONTINUITY LOG – CHAPTER 1: THE PRICE
Time and place: Nora deliberately travels by train, bus, and ferry to Isle of Thorn after finding Silas Vane's name in financial evidence connected to the Orlov operation.
Backstory established: Nora got an abused client released after an assault charge. The client killed her husband three weeks later. Mrs. Orlov blames Nora.
First attempt: Nora was stabbed at twenty-six. Security footage and her blood confirm the attack, but she woke without wounds.
Second attempt: Deliberate hit-and-run at twenty-eight. Severe injuries, six-week coma, lingering pain in pelvis and hip.
Third attempt: Intruder entered Nora's flat two days before Chapter 1, stole her evidence file, and forced her to flee through a bathroom window.
Why Nora goes to Thorn: She finds a payment from Orlov Holdings to Silas Vane and receives a warning that he finds people for powerful clients. She goes to confront him and learn whether he is hunting her.
What Nora knows by chapter end: Silas accepted payment from Orlov Holdings to deliver her alive. He knew the third attack was likely and expected it to push her toward Thorn. He has tracked her address, schedule, doctors, and past attacks. He has taken her phone and locked the house. The weather prevents immediate escape.
What Silas reveals: He did not give the Orlovs Nora's address. He knew they were losing patience. He believed she would survive and used the threat to direct her to Thorn. He admits this was control, not protection.
Physical state: Nora retains pain from the hit-and-run. Silas has a limp in the left leg. Nora cuts Silas beneath the ribs during their confrontation.
Open threads: Why Mrs. Orlov wants Nora alive. Why Silas chose not to deliver her. How he knew Nora would survive. Who Rowan is. Why the storm appeared as Nora opened the door. What is inside the file beyond the visible evidence.
